# Tournament Hub

**Used in:** [4. Register and build the site](../usage/register-and-build-the-site.md) ·
[6. Print](../usage/printables.md) · [7. Rehearse](../usage/rehearse.md)

Every S.A.G.E. event gets its own Tournament Hub — a public page linked
from a QR code posted at the venue. No app to install, no login to
remember — open it on a phone and everything below is already running.

The QR code sits on the venue's **hub board**, a 2 × 3 ft sign showing the
hub on three phones and how to use it. The board is the same at every event;
only its QR panel changes. See
[Usage § 6. Print](../usage/printables.md#the-hub-boards-qr-panel).

## For players: find your matches

The hub opens on **Match Finder**, its first tab — **Live Matches** and
**Standings** follow it once the day is live. After the first visit, the hub
remembers the last tab picked on that phone and reopens on it, as long as
that tab is showing for the day. Type your pair's names into
**Match Finder** and see your entire day at
once — every round, every opponent, in schedule order — without scrolling
past anyone else's matches. Search by either player's name or the full pair.
Before you search, Match Finder lists every match of the day, in schedule
order, so you can also just scroll to yours. The search box sits just above
the list, and on a phone it stays pinned to the top of the screen as you
scroll, so a new search is always in reach.

On a phone, running a search shows its results. Picking a suggestion, pressing
Enter or tapping **Find matches** closes the suggestions and the keyboard and
scrolls the page so your pair's matches start just under the pinned search box,
instead of leaving them below the banner. When the hub reopens on a saved search,
it scrolls to the results the same way as long as Match Finder is the tab it opens
on. Typing, clearing the box and the page refreshing with new scores never scroll.
On a computer the results are already on screen, so nothing scrolls, though
**Find matches** still closes the suggestions.

**The bar.** At the top of the page the banner (logos, tagline, date and venue, title,
tabs and sync line) is as it always was. Once it has scrolled out of view, a slim bar in
the event's dark colour stays pinned to the top of the screen, so switching tab never
means scrolling back up. On its left are the event's logo, its date and venue, and its
title. What is on the right depends on the screen:

- **On a phone**, a button naming the open tab (**Match Finder ☰**). It opens a menu over
  a dimmed page: the tabs, the open one marked, the day picker at an event with more than
  one day, and the sync line (**Sep 27 · Live · last synced 7:50:29 PM**). Picking a tab
  closes the menu, switches to it and scrolls to its content; picking a day loads it and
  returns to the top. The button (now a ×), a tap on the dimmed page or Escape closes the
  menu. Match Finder's search box, Standings' category headers and a dual meet's club
  score bar pin just under the bar.
- **On a computer** (900 px wide and over) there is no menu. The sync line, the day picker
  and the tabs sit in a row on the right of the bar, the open tab in the event's accent
  colour; below 1100 px the sync line is left out. The QR panel moves down under the bar,
  and the search box scrolls away with the page.

The bar goes away when the banner scrolls back into view. While no day is chosen or one
is loading there are no tabs, so there is no bar either. A team event's menu has its
fourth tab, Teams.

Above a pair's matches, a **summary card** says where the pair plays next and where it stands:

- A navy block that opens the card and answers *where and when do I play next?*
  It reads **Playing now** with the court (**Court 3**) while one of the pair's
  matches is on court, and otherwise **Next match** with the time and court
  (**2:50 PM · Court 4**) of the pair's next match that is not played. Under
  the time are the opponent (or *To be decided*, with the *Winner of Semifinal 2*
  or seat label when the opponent is known by where it comes from), the
  round (**Round robin**, **Semifinal**, **Final · Game 2**…), the match number,
  and, at a venue with more than one facility, the facility. A pair with
  nothing left to play has no block.
- Its category, and its bracket when the category has more than one
- Its rank in its round-robin bracket (**#2 of 5**), in the same order as
  Standings, with **W**, **L**, **PF** (points for), **PA** (points against)
  and **Q** (quotient). The rank reads **—** until the pair has played. Once
  every round-robin match of the category is in, it adds *round robin final*.
- Once the pair is in the playoffs, a row per round it has reached: the
  opponent (or *to be decided* with where that opponent comes from, such as
  *Winner of Semifinal 2*), then the match number and time, **Live · Court 2**
  while it is on court, or the result (**Won 11–8**). A twice-to-beat or
  best-of-3 final is one row over its games (**Won 2–1 (11–8, 6–11, 11–9)**).
- A pill summing it up: *In the Semifinals*, *Playing now*, *Champions*,
  *Runners-up*, *Bronze medal*, *4th place*, *Out in the Quarterfinals*, or
  *Did not advance* once the playoff seats are filled without them. A pair
  that takes bronze without playing (the round robin's #3 when there is no
  Bronze match) shows *Bronze medal* with *3rd in the round robin · no match*.

The card appears for standard and dual meet events. Before the day goes live it
is the next-match block alone, since no scores are shown yet; once it is live
the block sits above the pair's standing. A team event's Match Finder shows the
team's own results instead.

Each match card ("ticket") shows:

- The match number, as a dark tab in the card's top-left corner (`#42`), so it
  can be read off at a glance or called out to a court official
- The scheduled time and court
- A **Live** pill and pulsing dot while the match is actually in progress on
  a court
- A **Next Up** pill on whichever of your matches is next and not yet live
- The score, once it's in
- The category (for example *45+ Men's Doubles*) and which round it is (pool
  play, or a named playoff round like Quarterfinal, Semifinal, Bronze, Final)

## For spectators: watch it happen

**Live scores and court assignments** update on their own while play is
underway, usually within a few seconds of the score being entered — no
refreshing, no flagging down a volunteer to ask what's happening on Court 6.
**Live Matches** has a row per court, its match number in a tab at the top-left
of the match. A court whose match has both scores in, but which no one has given
its next match yet, reads **Done**: a check mark on an amber court in place of the
pulsing dot, the names greyed and the final score kept dark.

**Live standings** — win/loss records and rankings — update the same way,
sorted by division and category, so the board on your phone is never a stale
printout. At a standard tournament, a division whose round robin is split
into brackets shows one small table per bracket, labelled **Bracket 1**,
**Bracket 2**…, side by side. On a computer screen, the **1 col** button in
that division's header stacks them in one narrower column instead, and
**2 cols** puts them back.

A pair whose names aren't in the sheet yet still gets its round-robin row,
listed under its team code (`ND_1`, `ND_2`…) until the names are filled in.
Playoff spots that haven't been decided read **TBD**. Standings and the
schedule board keep the same size as names are filled in: every slot already
takes the space of two names, one line each, and a name too long for its line
ends in "…" (hover for the full names). A bronze won by
walkover (the round robin's #3 against a **BYE**, as in a twice-to-beat
category) has no **Bronze Battle** block at all: there is no matchup to show.
In a twice-to-beat or best-of-3 final, a game that is no longer needed (the #1
pair won twice-to-beat's game 1, or someone already has two wins) is
greyed out: in Standings, on the pair's Match Finder tickets, where it is
never **Next Up**, and on the schedule board. A category played as best-of-3
round-robin meetings greys a meeting's game 3 the same way once a pair has won
the first two, and its standings rank by meetings won.

**The playoffs as a bracket.** At a standard tournament, a category with two or
more playoff rounds has a **List | Bracket** switch at the end of its
**Playoffs** title. (Each standard category's card is in two titled
sections, **Round Robin** and **Playoffs**, with the brackets and the rounds
as the headings inside them.) **List**
is the matchup cards above; **Bracket** draws the playoffs as a chart, rounds
side by side with a line from each match to the seat its winner fills, and the
Bronze under the Final. One choice applies to every category and is
remembered on that device. Each card shows its match (`QF-2`), its court, and
**Done**, **Live** or the scheduled time, with the two seats' names and
scores, the winner shaded.

A seat that has no names yet says where it comes from instead:
**Winner of QF-2**, **Loser of SF-1**, the event's own label for a seat the
round robin fills (**Bracket winner**, **Best runner-up**), or **Qualifier**.
Once the sheet has the names, a seat the round robin filled also shows where
the pair finished, such as **Br 2 · #1** (first in bracket 2). The site never
shows a name the sheet does not have, and never works out who qualifies. An
event whose sheet predates the bracket columns shows the rounds side by side
with no lines, and an empty seat reads **TBD**.

On a computer the category's column stays the width its **1 col** / **2 cols**
toggle gives it, in List and Bracket alike, so switching never resizes the
board, and the toggle also sets how many rounds the chart shows at a time: two
side by side in a two-column card (Quarterfinals and Semifinals, then
Semifinals and the Final with the Bronze), one in a one-column card. A
two-round playoff in a two-column card is drawn whole. Where the chart does not
fit (a phone, in portrait too, or a narrow tablet), the card shows one round at
a time. Either way the cards stretch to fill the card's width, and the chart,
lines included, sits in a window you **swipe sideways** that always settles on
whole rounds: a row of round chips jumps to a round and lights every round in
view, and the window opens on the round being played. Under the chart or the window, **Full tree**
(on a tablet as well as a phone) opens the whole chart on a layer of its own,
scaled to fill the screen (shrunk on a phone, enlarged on a computer): pinch
to zoom, drag to move, double-tap to zoom in on a spot or back out, **Fit** to
start again, and the Back gesture or **✕**
to close it. Its cards are wider than the chart's, with each player of a pair on
a line of their own, so full names show. Zoomed far out, a card shows surnames
in larger type.

Each round-robin table shows a pair's **W** (wins), **L** (losses), **PF**
(points for: every point it has scored in its played matches), **PA** (points
against: every point scored against it) and **Q** (quotient, PF ÷ PA). A match
against a BYE counts for neither.

Within each round-robin table, pairs are ranked by **wins**, then
**head-to-head** (among pairs level on wins, whoever won the matches
between them), then **quotient**. When three or more pairs are level and
their results against each other go in a circle, quotient decides. This is
the website's ranking only: the scoring workbook picks who advances
to the playoffs by wins and then quotient, so on a head-to-head tie the
two can differ.

**Where a table stands, and how a tie was broken.** Once the day is live, every
round-robin table's heading carries a pill: **In progress · 6/10** while some of the
table's round-robin meetings are unplayed (here six of ten are played), and **Final**
once every one is. A bracket's pill sits in its **Bracket 2** label, a table with no
brackets has it in its **Round Robin** heading, and a dual meet's one table per club has
one in its heading. A best-of-3 meeting counts as one meeting, played once a pair has
two games, and a meeting against a BYE is not counted. Before the day is live no pill
shows.

A pair level on wins with another also carries a pill under its names saying how the
table placed it (pairs that have not played get none):

- **Tie · won head-to-head 11–5** and **Tie · lost head-to-head 5–11**: two pairs
  separated by the match between them, the pair's own score first. A best-of-3 meeting
  reads in games (*2–1*) and a double round robin in meetings won (*2–0*);
- **3-way tie · head-to-head**: three or more pairs level, and this pair's wins against
  the others differ from every other pair's;
- **Tie · on quotient** (**3-way tie · on quotient** for three or more): level on
  head-to-head too, so the quotient placed the pair. Pairs at a dual meet's club table
  never meet, so their ties always read this way;
- **Tied**, in yellow: level on head-to-head and quotient as well, so the table keeps the
  workbook's order.

The pills describe the website's order. The workbook still picks who advances by wins and
then quotient, so on a head-to-head tie it can disagree with the table.

**A row opens that pair's matches.** On a standard or dual-meet Hub each named
round-robin row in Standings ends with a **›** after the quotient. Tapping the row, or
pressing Enter on it with the keyboard, switches to Match Finder with that pair searched:
the summary card, with the pair's next match first, and its matches under it. On a phone
the page lands on the results just under the search box; on a computer it goes to the
search box, so the results are in view wherever Standings had been scrolled to. A slot
with no names yet (an unseeded slot, **TBD**, or a pair listed under its team code) has no
**›** and does nothing, and neither does a playoff matchup. A team event's Standings
and Control Center's are unchanged.

**A podium on a finished category.** Once a category's result is known, its Standings card opens with a
**Podium** card before the round-robin tables: gold, silver and bronze with the pairs' names, and under gold
the final, for example *Beat Makeen Fernandez / Noelle Mercado 11–7 in the final*. A final played as a series
(twice-to-beat or best-of-3) has no single score, so its line reads *Beat … in the final*. A dual meet's names are
followed by the club (*Keren Kenizzite / Shaira Tarlit · Pickle & Friends Community*). The podium appears only when it
is certain: the final and the bronze match are both played with a clear winner, or the category has no bronze match
(then two places show), or the category was decided by its round robin alone and every match is in (then there is
no final line). A category still playing, a final or bronze that is tied on points, or a bronze not yet played shows
no podium. It is the same podium as Control Center's Awards tab. Standard and dual-meet events;
Control Center's Standings do not draw it.

**Club Showdown** *(dual-meet events only)* — a running head-to-head win
count between the two clubs sits front and center on Standings, updated
after every match, with whichever club is ahead visually highlighted.

## Team events

A team event's page is a Tournament Hub like the others, with a fourth
tab, Teams. Its pair labels (MD, WD, XD…) are the event's own, set in the
event's settings. A team event has named teams instead of pairs. Two teams meet in a
**matchup** — four matches (men's doubles, women's doubles and two mixed
doubles) between the same two teams — and **team names appear everywhere a
team does**: Standings, Match Finder, Live Matches and the schedule board.
The letter codes the workbook uses (`A`, `B`…) are not shown here;
Control Center still shows them beside each team name. When the event's teams
have colours, as at the Millennium Invitational, each team name has a mark in front
of it, the team's colour on a shape of its own (a circle, a star, a diamond…), and
each matchup card shades from one team's colour to the other's. The shape tells
the teams apart for anyone who can't tell the colours apart. The Hub, the schedule
board and Control Center all show them.

**Reading a matchup card.** The header gives the stage (a bracket,
Quarterfinal, Semifinal, Bronze or Final), the two time slots and the courts. Under it, both team names
face each other around the **matchup score**, with the **pair count** (for
example *pairs 3–1*, the matches each team won) in small type beneath. Below
that, one row per match shows its number in a small dark tab, the pair (MD, WD or XD), the players on each
side (one per line, first players level with each other and second players
level, however a name wraps) and the score. A match being played right now carries a live dot and its
court. A pill marks a matchup that is *In progress* (from the moment one of its
matches is on court, before any score is in), *Final* or a *Tie*, and
the team that won a finished matchup is marked **Winner**.

**Why a matchup is won on total points.** The team with more points across
all four matches wins the matchup — not the team that wins more matches. A team
can win three matches 11–9 and lose the fourth 0–11, and still lose the
matchup 33–38. That is why the pair count is only secondary information. If
both teams finish on the same total, the matchup is a **tie** (equal points),
shown as *Tie* with no winner named.

**Events scored on games won.** An event whose `events.json` entry sets
`teamScoring: "games"` decides a matchup differently: the team that wins more of
the four matches wins it, and if each wins the same number, the team with more
total points does. Level on both, the matchup is a **tie**. The card's large
score is then the matches won (*3 – 1*) with the points beneath (*points
35–38*). Under each team's name the card gives that team's **PF**, **PA** and
**quotient** (PF ÷ PA) in the matchup (*PF 35 · PA 38 · Q 0.9211*), and Live
Matches shows the matches won as the running matchup score. The Bracket table
shows **W**, **L**, **PF**, **PA** and **Quotient** across the round robin, and
is ranked by matches won. Two teams level on wins are separated by head-to-head
wins, then the head-to-head quotient (points for ÷ points against in their
matchup); three or more by their quotient, with two still level going to the
two-team rules. Teams level after that stay in the workbook's order, and once the
round robin is over they share the higher rank and read **Tied · playoff game**
(two teams) or **Tied · playoff** (three or more): the organiser's
playoff settles it, and the qualifiers typed into the workbook's playoff slots
show who goes on. An event can skip the head-to-head quotient, as Ladies' Dink does:
two teams that split their matchup 1–1 then read **Tied · playoff game** at once.
When the playoff is entered as a tiebreaker row, its result settles the tie and the pill
clears; its points and wins don't appear in the table.

An event's brackets may be lettered. The workbook's `bracket` column then reads
`A`, `B`… and the Hub titles each table **Bracket A**, **Bracket B**… in letter
order, with Match Finder grouping the teams the same way.

**A playoff matchup's tiebreaker.** At an event whose playoff matchups are two
games, a matchup that ends 1–1 plays one more game, the **Tiebreaker** (**TB**):
each team nominates one pair. The card lists it as a third row, *Lineup not set*
until the operator types the nominated players in. A matchup won 2–0 never plays
it, so its card doesn't list one, and the matchup reads *Final* without it.

**Lineups shown when the organiser says.** An event can hold its lineups back
until each round: at the Millennium Invitational a match's players appear on the
Hub and the schedule board only once match control ticks that matchup's
**Show lineup** box in the workbook's `MatchUps` tab. Until then the match reads *Lineup not set*.

**Playoff placeholders.** Until the organiser types a team into a playoff slot,
the slot reads *Seed 1 · TBD*. An event can name its slots instead: at the
Millennium Invitational the Final reads *Rank 1 · TBD* v *Rank 2 · TBD* and the
Bronze *Rank 3 · TBD* v *Rank 4 · TBD*, the bracket places that will fill them.

**Standings** show a table per bracket, ranked by the organizer's
tiebreakers, in order:

1. **Points scored** across the team's bracket matches.
2. **Quotient** — points scored divided by points conceded.
3. **Head-to-head points** — among the teams still level, the points each
   scored in its matches against the others.
4. **Pair wins** — the number of matches the team won in its bracket.

Teams level on all four stay in the workbook's order. Before the first result
the tables list the teams without rank numbers. Eight teams reach the
**quarterfinals**, seeded 1–8 and paired 3 v 6, 1 v 8, 2 v 7 and 4 v 5. An
operator enters who they are by typing their letters into the workbook
against each seed; the site marks those teams **Advances** and never works it
out for itself. Until a seed is filled in, its card reads *Seed 3 · TBD* and so
on. Below the tables come the playoff matchups — Quarterfinals, Semifinals,
Bronze, Final — then each bracket's matchups behind a collapsible heading. On
a computer screen the quarterfinals sit two by two and the semifinals share
one row, while Bronze and Final each take a full row; a phone shows one
matchup per row.

Once the day is live each bracket's title carries the same status pill, **In progress · 3/6**
or **Final**, counting the bracket's matchups (a matchup is played once it reads *Final* or a
tie). A team table has no tie pills: its ranking and its own **Tied · playoff** pill are as
above.

Once the Final is played, **Standings** open with the event's **Podium**: the Final's winner, its loser and the
Bronze matchup's winner, by team name, and under the winner the Final's score, from the winner's side: points
(*Beat Can i buy you a DINK? 32–26 in the final*), or pairs won at an event that ranks on games (*Beat Pink 2–1 in the final*;
when the Final is level on games and decided on points, *Beat Pink 37–35 on points in the final*). With no Bronze
matchup the podium has two places. It stays out until the Final is played, and while the Final or the Bronze is tied on
points, so the organizer has to settle it first.

**Searching by team or player.** Match Finder takes a team name or a player's
name. A team result lists every matchup that team plays, in schedule order,
with the team on the left and *Next up* on its first matchup still to be
played. A player result lists each match they play, each inside its matchup
card with only their own match showing. With nothing typed, Match Finder
shows the teams, then every matchup of the day, ordered by match number: a
matchup whose matches are #3, #8, #9 and #17 sits where #3 would. Tapping a
team runs its search, and on a phone shows its results as above (on a computer the page
scrolls back to the top). Each matchup card in a team result or that list shows its
matches, with a **Hide matches** button that folds them away (and **Show
matches** to bring them back); the page keeps the choice as scores arrive.

**Teams.** A **Teams** tab lists every team's roster, grouped by bracket: one
card per team with its name and player count, which opens to show the players
with their level and gender, lowest level first. **Expand all** opens every
card. It shows before the day goes live too, since rosters give nothing away,
and it updates when the workbook's `Teams` tab changes. Match
Finder uses the roster as well: a team result starts with that team's roster,
and a player can be found by name before any lineup is in, showing the team
they play for and their teammates. A player's result shows their level and
gender beside their name.

**Lineup not set.** A team captain enters each matchup's players in the
workbook before it is played. Until then its match rows read *Lineup not set*
(the schedule board says *Lineup TBD*). A player is still found by name from
the roster, but their matches appear once their lineup is in. The same player may play a different pair in each matchup.

## At the venue: the schedule board

A wall display for a screen at the venue, not a phone. It is unlisted: nothing
here links to it, and an operator launches it from Control Center. See
[Schedule board](schedule-board.md).

## What you *won't* see here

Tournament Hub is read-only and always shows the current state of play —
it has no sign-in, no editing, and (before an event goes live) hides scores
and standings entirely rather than showing incomplete data. The tools
operators use to run the event — Mission Control, the Awards podium/export,
resyncing a facility — live in a separate console. See
[Control Center](control-center.md).
