    • [1.0.0] — 2026-10-08

    Changelog¶

    What changed in these docs, release by release. The other pages describe the system as it is; the history of how the docs got there lives here.

    How the docs are versioned¶

    The docs carry a version, MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH, shown in the footer of every page. What a reader would notice decides which number goes up:

    Bump When Examples
    MAJOR A page or nav section is removed, renamed or moved, so a bookmark or a link from another repo stops working. Splitting a tab in two; deleting a page and sharing its content out.
    MINOR Something new to read: a new page or section, a page updated for a change in the product, or a new spec or a spec moving status folder. A features page for a new tool; a feature's new behaviour; a spec moving to implemented/.
    PATCH A correction that changes nothing in the product: a wrong fact, a broken link, a typo, clearer wording. Fixing a menu name; repointing a link.

    A spec's URL includes its status folder, so a status move is MINOR, not MAJOR. Cite specs by filename, as the Specs index describes, and a status move breaks nothing.

    Releasing a version¶

    Every change that reaches main carries its version, in the same commit (or the last commit of its pull request):

    1. Add an entry at the top of this page: ## [x.y.z] — YYYY-MM-DD, dated in Manila time. A pull request with several changes gets one entry, with the largest bump among them.
    2. Set the same version in mkdocs.yml's copyright line.

    The deploy workflow refuses to publish when the two disagree.

    An entry groups its lines under Added, Changed, Removed and Fixed. History records facts taken out of the pages because they describe the past rather than the system: renames, earlier versions, measurements of a design that has been replaced.

    [2.65.0] — 2026-11-14¶

    Changed¶

    • Court Control from Control Center goes from an outline to a build spec with every decision settled, written for a no-context build. Operators only; on when the event's scoreEntry is; the court's own cell in Live Matches is the button (✎ Set match / ✎ Change). PUT /v3/…/courts/{court}/match, in a new src/courts/ folder, finds a court's row by the number in column B: the team and dual-meet workbooks start their courts at row 6, not 5. The console's side is a courtCell option of views/live-matches.js, a new domain/courts.js and views/court-dialog.js, so the Hub and scorer page are unchanged. The score dialog's "put the next match on this court" prompt is a later phase, outlined only.

    [2.61.0] — 2026-11-12¶

    Changed¶

    • Calculator team format is revised against the calculator as it is now, the two team events since it was written (the Millennium Invitational and Ladies' Dink), and a draft plan for a two-day team event on 5–6 December. The playoff starts at a chosen round, from a Round of 16 down to Bronze + Final straight from the standings, and always ends in a Bronze and a Final. A tiebreaker game, in the playoffs only or in every matchup, adds a slot to each matchup or round that has one. The break before the playoffs is a checkbox, ticked at 25 minutes. Matches on court at once gains a hint that matches sharing a player can't share a slot, and a plan whose matchups leave courts idle (3 matches 2 at a time, or a group-stage tiebreaker) gets an alert that the group stage could run shorter than the model's estimate. Its worked examples reproduce all three team events' finishes (4:05, 9:00 and 9:40 PM) and the December draft's Choice and Select divisions (7:50 and 2:50 PM); its Prime division rests teams between matchups, which the model leaves to the break. The owner answered all ten of its questions. It is written to be built with no other context: the calculator's 29 edits by anchor, a nine-case unit test of calcTeam against those examples, the docs changes and the browser checks, all dry-run on a copy of the calculator.

    Fixed¶

    • The same spec said a team plan imported by an older copy of the calculator reports "no categories found". It imports as a standard plan and keeps the categories on screen.
    • The same spec reused the after-10 PM alert unchanged, whose one more court figure is the standard format's arithmetic. For a team plan it now suggests the courts of one more lane, worked out with the team model.

    [2.60.0] — 2026-11-12¶

    Fixed¶

Its Standings, Match Finder, Teams tab, facility progress, Awards and team sections named functions the console no longer has; they now name the engine's (createStandings, createFinder, createTeams, stageTablesHTML, buildPodiums(model) and the rest) and say what stays in the page. The tab row has up to seven tabs, Awards is the second, and the page loads qrcode-generator from cdnjs for Show QR.
    • Event registry schema: type is read by every engine page and, for rosters, by sage-tools-api; archived also turns score entry off in the API; date drives go-live, progress and both link kinds; facility workbooks are Restricted, so a published sheet ID opens nothing.
    • API: thirteen of the seventeen /v3 routes have a frozen twin, not sixteen; the people list takes a desk token too, as Auth now says.
    • Sync pipeline: the site's copy of the completion rules is the engine's lib/v1/domain/ with its parity test; a day's trigger runtime matches the measurements (50–60 minutes, 105 at PickleDrive); the SAGE menu lists Open Bracket Generator; score entry's own SheetsClient and the lazy scoresheet import (Scoresheet pipeline) are in src/app.mjs; live diagnostics carry switch and active (also Deployment).
    • Architecture names sage-docs and the AttendanceSignals port.
    • Site engine's layout lists every module.
    • Schedule board: a card's fourth state, not needed, and where its stage regexes live.
    • Finished events' paths point at events/archives/ in Adding a new event, Event attendance and the implemented specs' index.
    • The changelog's links to the Ladies' Dink spec point at implemented/.

    [2.59.0] — 2026-11-12¶

    Added¶

    • Ranking rules, a not-started spec: an event's standings ordered by a list of named rules in events.json, with blocks by group size and an optional restart, applied to each tied group in turn. The standard, "points" and "games" rankers become presets with parity tests. Display only. Its decisions are taken; it has no build date.

    [2.58.0] — 2026-11-12¶

    Added¶

    • The event spec template gains §1.3, several facilities, or several days: each facility's workbook, match-number range and sync setup, and the board's one day, with the owner choosing between pushing it each evening and an engine phase that lets it pick its day. It also gains Part 3, the engine phase's skeleton to insert before the board: tests first, the API's half when the played or BYE rules change, the documentation, and the pushes, API first.

    Changed¶

    • The event spec template's Phases 2 and 3 carry every command written out, as Phase 1 does. The line-endings table is replaced by git ls-files --eol, read when the spec is written. The contrast checks give each pair its WCAG line (4.5:1 body, 3:1 large text) and what a shortfall means. Part 1's §1.3–§1.7 become §1.4–§1.8.

    [2.57.0] — 2026-11-12¶

    Added¶

    • The event spec template, at the root of Specs: one template for every event type. Part 1 says how to write an event spec: gather the workbook, the pubmat and the owner's answers first; build in phases (the board with events.json, the Hub, the rest, and an engine phase first when one is needed); anchor every edit on one unique line, state each file's line endings and never use sed -i; check the Hub at 1024px as well as 375 and 1280; and dry-run the spec in a detached worktree. Part 2 is the skeleton, in the UPAP spec's shape, with Standard, Team and Dual meet blocks. The specs index points to it.

The coordinator, a SAGE member, prepares the event: plan, workbooks, draw, rosters, printing. The developer, a SAGE member, does everything technical. The operators are the SAGE members on site on the day, and they set up and run the venue screens. Scorers and desk staff can be SAGE members or the organizer's volunteers.
    • The operators' three jobs on the day: calling matches (Court Control, calling matches to courts, the qualifier draws), scores and playoff names, and players and the organizer. Jobs can overlap, but the operator calling matches never also coordinates with the organizer. Run the day says which job does each part of play.

    • On the day, an operator types the qualifier draw, team lineups, playoff seeds and late changes into the workbook; the organizer decides them. Features and Technical say the same.

    History¶

    • Control Center was first called Match Control. The file, the URL and every reference moved together; tools/match-control.html stays as a redirect stub. The console and Awards tab specs predate the rename.
    • Pickle for Sight's attendance is the earlier, per-workbook version: an attendance.gs web app in each workbook, one row per pair slot, no desk links and no Attendance tab. It was removed from sage-tools-api; its last version is apps-script/attendance.gs at commit 198f02c. Every later event uses the API's ATTENDANCE tab, which the API creates in a workbook made before the generators added it.
    • The Bracket Generator replaced four near-identical copies: two event-site templates and one live event's copy, which had drifted to a pre-S.A.G.E. palette. That event's copy became a redirect stub; archived events' copies are frozen. Adding keep-apart groups left a draw without groups byte-for-byte the same.
    • The generators replaced duplicating the last event's workbook and find-replacing every category key, court number and team code by hand. The dual-meet generator's spec was written before the workbook was read closely, hence its §13; every bug found while building the generator was a block one row off, which is why it logs its layout.
    • The time-based sync trigger. Before the lock-based sync, sheets-sync.gs scheduled a one-shot runIfSettled trigger from every edit. Measured on the Piggleball workbook on 1 October 2026, the delay from the last edit to the sync starting was 119, 70, 74, 22 and 108 s across five bursts: a median of about 74 s against the 3 s intended. Each onEditInstallable run took 1.3–3.3 s, mostly deleting and recreating triggers, and parallel edits raced on that and left stray triggers, so a workbook could sync twice. The lock-based sync replaced it; a non-holder run takes 0.6–2.2 s.
    • Live push replaced polling GitHub Pages as the main delivery path. With polling alone, a published score took about 35–40 s to reach a viewer.
    • The PickleDrive workbook was trimmed on 5 October 2026: the StackCache tab, a rewritten matchup family and 15 named functions removed, with every published value unchanged.
    • completedAt is newer than the first published snapshots. A snapshot from before it uses the per-browser syncedAt fallback.
    • The team template. PickleDrive Club One Year Celebration, the first team event, was hand-built as events/pickledrive-anniversary-2026/ before the team template existed.
    • sage-tools-api 3.0.0 restructured the service. Before it, the service was a feature-sliced, layered monolith with hand-wired constructor injection: each service was coded against the concrete object it received, routes did their own auth and error mapping, and Server imported every feature. /v1 was the partial REST surface of the 2.x releases.
    • Control Center's Match Finder refresh used to re-run the search box's text, so pausing mid-word swapped the list for "Several pairs match" and the pinned search box lost its scroll. It redraws the search that was run.
    • The Tournament Calculator's legend used to infer extra rounds from plan.byes, and said "no preliminary rounds needed" above a chart showing two.
    • The service-account share inherited from 1. TOURNAMENTS was confirmed on 2026-10-08: Piggleball's workbook there lists the account as Editor.
